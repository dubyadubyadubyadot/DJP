Memoria Future . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

Dear Enkidu, missing you
You Champion of death
Hidden moon, I cry to you
For any heartbeat to beat in my breath
Your fire’s lick
Once kept my body burning hot for you
But now my knuckles groan
Your sword marched strong beside my sword
I’ve bathed in valleys of your fur
You never-dying bull
Be somewhere near
Enjoy your healthful brambles
I’d drink your cud for you
To have you here
And I would name it milk
Without you
My sheath sings its loneliness
These fighting hands are clenched for death
Every beast I deem below you
Each god too
Except that god we make together
That One’s true
Forgive me for my wanting
This desert’s cold tonight
I pray to you
Strong tree
Be giving in your green
Twist out your wisdom
Tell me
How can I speak your language?
How will you know my knots?
I am lonely in a flowering desert
Teach me to leaf like you
Teach me to swing in my branches
And then I, my sword aside, will bring you
That small bright gift my body brings

Broadway Junction . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

Why did I wait? At Broadway junction I couldn’t release the day My life ending with the sun Out of time I kicked up crucible and construction dust Forged a tunnel through space with the bricks of my body Slagged and heaved Across the hot cement and lecherous rust And plugged my ears from the porous tar-slicked moan of Construction City Out of time and barely through the door Somehow I found the Iron Gates at Broadway Junction Found the muscles of my arms to heave my phone above my waist And flex my thumb to double click and take a picture of my Face While somewhere behind: my rubbery flesh, the phone Accepts my anxious stare then seems to gleam a bit As it dings me through Its swinging Carousel Cops, cocked and thick, slime like slugs nearby Algae on the floor Out of time and running late my mind sprinted away But What gave me feet at Broadway Junction? As taught and timorous as Paul’s lucky thunder My body ran to catch my mind past the debodied Sacrificed to Job and Sponsor They Flow in that rising current of The escalator’s drain. Some dart like dragon flies But the Hunting Breath is coming for us all Who brought my eyes to Broadway Junction? Pigeons and smokers lopsided and waiting Find one more hit beneath the metal grates of stairs Vape suckers indiscreet sip from the timer of fate Facetimers file complaint and complaint of the friend Or the friend of the boss or the boss or the boss who just can’t— won’t— Get it Hot and molten entropy drains to the dusk’s beckoning fingertips Busses in the outer lots lay like beetles in the branch Or corn or cacao in cluster What is this tingling breath in my breast? What is this thin click in my wrist? What are these glowing eyes and what is this mind who sings ideas into songs? Spilling into dusk the city wings its flight My mind floats from all civility and civilization Reaches past the above-ground platform And sees Above the field of busses Above the abandoned edifii Above the swallowing boulevard Gravestones crop the hill And beyond that bed of marrow Patient cedars wait Light breeze, this dusk Leaves nearby laugh and wave in warning They know what time the tide will come To Broadway Junction The ground that grabs my feet rattles with a cough Rattles like the hangry bird Or a warlock seeking vengeance Hairs on my neck like protestant hands reach for something to Grab Wind bellows with the trains encroaching thunder What gave my mind permission to imagine past these rattling frames? Destroy these trellised toothpick tracks, teetering off a tilting earth Destroy these fumeslicked bus-shells, flatbeds, trucks and semis Destroy each monster of our labor’s making Rip the gauzy haze that keeps our lives Productive Strip us of all Product and Productivity And what is left? And what brings me such ideas? Is it you, God? The doors open to swallow I shiver

Adrienne's Hymn . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

My spine clacks
Heavy with commuters
Carries me somewhere
Home-bound
Wrapped in ropes
Of my eating flesh
It asks to be let out
Like that word
Outside my tongue
Just hovering above
The hyperbaric chamber of my mind
With one shoulder reach
I might set it free
Whip it like a snake
Against a dust threaded rock
But no
Something’s sharp and arctic in me
Fossil baked in an unforgetting country
Still waiting for its prey
This spine knows a mother’s womb
Has felt the stars pour light to dust
It catches where my shoulders lift my body to my mind
It speaks in clacketing rumbles:
Slow down brimmed box
Reach into each part of you this
Morning arrives like an unplanned birth
And this spine’s been carrying the load
Lift up
Reach out
Bow down
Bend back
Reach here for what you thought you left behind
It’s nearby
Just out of touch
Your body’s kept it
Pocketed in a hip
Slipped under the steel wings of your shoulder blades
Your body is the mother to the child of your mind
Hold it
She knows more than you could
Dare to Witness

A Tree From my Country . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

A tree from my country died last night
I found it thunder struck and bared of bark
On the back end of a walk out of Brooklyn
I stood more alone
One less branch broke from my body
My skin shuddered bare
The stars behind the thunder didn’t dare to bring an eye
The Tree sat unshrouded
The trunk didn’t try
The birds washed away with the current
And there was no one to call
I of the Throng
Aghast and affronted
In all my human strength and spirit
Could only think to Look
And to etch you here with some dribbles from my tongue
But not to weep No Not to weep
Could you hear it too?
Above that branch?
Saturn with his giant Mouth and Throat—
Hester twines her lyre—
Calling to incessance—
Bells rung
